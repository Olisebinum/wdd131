Building an Emergency Fund

Your financial safety net — covering unexpected expenses without going into debt. Aim for 3–6 months of essential living costs in a liquid, separate account.

  1. Calculate your monthly essential costs (rent, food, transport, utilities).
  2. Multiply by 3 to get your minimum target.
  3. Open a dedicated account separate from your spending account.
  4. Automate a transfer the day after salary lands.
  5. Start with ₦5,000/month if needed — consistency beats amount.
  6. Do not touch it unless it is a genuine emergency.

Best options: Kuda high-interest savings, PiggyVest SafeLock, or any account offering ≥10% p.a.

💸

Savings Strategies That Work

The best savings strategy is the one you will actually stick to. Four approaches proven to work on Nigerian salaries.

  1. Pay yourself first: Move savings before spending anything else.
  2. Automate sweeps: Scheduled transfers remove willpower from the equation entirely.
  3. Goal-based pots: Name each pot — Emergency, School Fees, Travel — to reduce the urge to dip in.
  4. 1% rule: Increase your savings rate by 1% every time you get a pay rise. You will not notice it.
  5. Round-up savings: Some apps round purchases to the nearest ₦500 and save the difference automatically.

Budgeting in a High-Inflation Economy

Nigeria's inflation makes budgeting harder but far more important. Prices shift monthly — your budget must adapt with them.

  1. Update your budget at the start of every month — never copy last month's figures blindly.
  2. Prioritise essentials first; inflation hits food and transport hardest.
  3. Build a 5–10% "price shock" buffer inside your needs bucket for unexpected price jumps.
  4. Track spending in naira, not foreign currency equivalents.
  5. Consider dollar-denominated savings (PiggyVest Dollar Flex) as an inflation hedge on part of your income.
